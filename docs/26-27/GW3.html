The Week in Words
GW3 — an editorial nobody asked for
The Disgraces
Vignesh Ramesh takes the wooden spoon on 30, a full 19 below an average that was itself nothing to boast about, and he got there by handing the armband to B.Fernandes for 4 — the second time in three gameweeks a Vigtorious captain has produced next to nothing. Sahil Vora found the more expensive route to the same place: a -4 hit that finished -15 down on the transfers, against a stand-pat score of 54 that would have landed him in the week's top ten. He took 42 instead. Stephen Rudd scored a perfectly respectable 60 and left 24 on his bench, the largest single pile of unused points anyone has managed this season. And the two long-running cases show no sign of resolving themselves: Martin Borizanov added another 11 to a bench that has now swallowed 52 points in three gameweeks, while Amit Putty contributed 16 more towards his own total of 48. Between them they have benched exactly one hundred points since August, which is a full gameweek and a half of football watched from a sofa.
The Shockers
Nnaemeka Ezugwu produced the week's best return, 66, and he did it without anything exotic — just a 15-point week from Mitchell, a player almost nobody else in the league thought to own. It moved Project X 8 places, from seventeenth to ninth, the sharpest climb of the season. Two points behind came Rahul Vahi on 64, which in most weeks would have been the headline. But the week belonged, again, to Bluehomie (YouTube), who played a Wildcard — a third chip in three gameweeks, after the Bench Boost and the Triple Captain. This column stated with some confidence last week that he had nothing left in the drawer until October. He had, in fact, a wildcard, and he used it to swap Barry for Isak and bank 11 points from that move alone. He remains top of the table. The drawer, it turns out, still contains a Free Hit and a considerable amount of quiet satisfaction.
The Title Race
Bluehomie (YouTube) leads on 248, has now held top spot for two weeks, and has stretched the gap to 18. It was actually Eze is second on 230, which is precisely where Jay Curtis has finished every single week of this season — second after Gameweek 1, second after Gameweek 2, second again now. Rice to meet you is third on 227 and Quantum of Tzolis fourth on 225, though Akash Patel's campaign is quietly becoming a study in armbands: top of the league after the opening weekend, fourth ever since, and now the owner of two blank captaincies in three gameweeks. Son of a Doge has gone tenth, sixteenth, seventeenth, and wildcarded in a week that cost him five points against simply standing still — a side that led this league for eighteen weeks last season now hunting for the floor. At the bottom, Vigtorious on 154 and Sporting Club MarRow on 151 are pulling clear in the wrong direction. 35 gameweeks remain, which is a very long time to spend eighteen points behind a man who is visibly enjoying himself.
The Nerd Corner
For the first time this season the league lost to the rest of the world: 49.4 against a global average of 51. That is a swing of more than twenty points from Gameweek 2, when the comparison read 98.7 against 79, and it is the clearest evidence yet that August's superiority was a fixture list rather than a room full of geniuses. The template XI scored 44 and 12 of 23 managers beat it, up sharply from 4 of 23 a week ago — when the football is hard, thinking for oneself starts to pay. Chip usage has become a stampede: 18 chips in three gameweeks, including seven Triple Captains in this one, every single one of them on Haaland, every single one returning exactly 27. Seven managers spent their most valuable chip to arrive at an identical number to one another, which is a curious way to gain an advantage. The luck ledger has changed hands as well — It was actually Eze now runs 22 points clear of expected points for the season, inheriting the title from a North London Forever side that has since returned to earth.
