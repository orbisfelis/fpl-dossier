The Week in Words
GW5 — an editorial nobody asked for
The Disgraces
For three weeks this column has been keeping a running inventory of Bluehomie (YouTube)'s chip drawer, noting each time that a Free Hit remained in it. He played the Free Hit. Bluehomie (YouTube) would have scored 49 by leaving the team exactly as it was, scored 34 instead, and surrendered 15 points for the privilege — the most expensive act of intervention anyone has managed this season, and it has ended a reign of three weeks at the summit. Akash Patel took the wooden spoon with 30, 18 below the league average, which completes a curious arc for a Quantum of Tzolis side that led this league after the opening weekend and has now finished first, fourth, fourth, fifth and last. Stephen Rudd handed the armband to Marmoush for 4, the week's worst captaincy, and Vignesh Ramesh left 27 points on his bench, the largest unused pile of the campaign. Sunny Sharma paid a -4 to finish 2 down on the transfers, and Jay Curtis played an entire Wildcard to end up 7 points worse off. Four managers spent something this week — a chip, a hit, a transfer, an opinion — and all four finished poorer for it.
The Shockers
Kris McCarthy captained Semenyo for 34, the best armband decision anybody made, and posted 66. This is the same Sporting Club MarRow that took last week's wooden spoon with 50 and whose residence in the bottom two this column described, with some confidence, as less a slump than a settled address. It remains twenty-third, but it has just outscored 20 of the 22 sides above it. Martin Borizanov produced the week's highest total, 69, and climbed 6 places, the sharpest rise of the round. Jay Curtis salvaged something from his Wildcard with the transfer of the week — Groß in for M.Sangaré, worth 14 — and Vignesh Ramesh extracted 22 from a Free Hit, which would have been the story of most gameweeks and was instead entirely obscured by what he left on the bench. But the week belonged to Evros Stephanou, who scored 67 and moved from fifth to first. Calafiori Dreamin' began this season eighteenth.
The Title Race
Calafiori Dreamin' leads this league for the first time, on 368, having been eighteenth after the opening weekend and fifth as recently as last Sunday. The margin is 1 point. Son of a Doge sits on 367 and has now gone sixteenth, third and second in the space of a fortnight, which is the most violent upward correction the table has seen. Pamparam is third on 362, 6 back, and Bluehomie (YouTube) fourth on 360 — deposed, 8 adrift, and out one Free Hit. Martin's Team completes the group on 357 after the week's best score. Five sides are separated by 11 points with 33 gameweeks remaining, which is the tightest this league has been at any point this season and, on the evidence of the last fortnight, means very little about where any of them will finish. At the other end Vigtorious on 272 and Sporting Club MarRow on 267 remain the bottom two for a fourth consecutive week, though Sporting Club MarRow outscored three of the four sides above the leader's shoulder this week.
The Nerd Corner
The league lost to the wider world again, though only barely: 47.7 against a global average of 48. The genuinely striking number is the template XI, which managed 34 and was beaten by 20 of 23 managers — a complete inversion of Gameweek 4, when it scored 78 and only 8 of 23 cleared it. Last week this column concluded that when the obvious picks all deliver, thinking for oneself is a net cost; this week the obvious picks delivered nothing at all and almost everybody beat them, which suggests the lesson was about the fixtures rather than the thinking. The luck ledger continues to run one way: Son of a Doge is now 60 points clear of expected points across the season, a surplus that has grown every week since it was first noted and which sits underneath a side currently one point off the lead. The two captaincy records flagged a fortnight ago are unchanged, with North London Forever and Quantum of Tzolis still on 2 blank armbands apiece. The pattern worth watching is that two of this week's three highest scores came from twenty-third and fifth, which is not how leagues normally consolidate.
