The Week in Words
GW2 — an editorial nobody asked for
The Disgraces
Kris McCarthy has produced the season's first genuine catastrophe, and he built it himself, from parts. Sporting Club MarRow scored 68 in a week the league averaged 98.7, paid an -8 hit for the privilege, and finished -16 down on the transfers once the players he sold had finished scoring without him. Standing perfectly still would have returned 83. That is 15 points fed directly into a shredder, and it leaves him tied at the very bottom on 113 with Releven Tigers FC, who at least had the decency to get their wooden spoon out of the way in Gameweek 1. Martin Borizanov is meanwhile running a bit that has stopped being funny to him: 21 points on the Martin's Team bench this week, after 20 last week, which is 41 abandoned in two gameweeks by a man sitting 22 off the lead. Started, that bench would have him top of the league by nineteen. And a word for Amit Putty, fourth after Gameweek 1 despite leaving 23 in the dugout — this column noted at the time what he could have had, and this week the bill arrived: 80 points and a fall to thirteenth. The template XI scored 109; 19 of 23 managers were beaten by a side that required no thought at all.
The Shockers
Bluehomie (YouTube) did not win the gameweek so much as annex it. 127 points, 11 clear of the field, powered by a Triple Captain that turned B.Fernandes's 23 into 69 — the single most decisive call anyone has made this season. It carried the team from fifth to the summit in one afternoon. What makes it remarkable is the company it keeps: this is the same manager who burned a Bench Boost in Gameweek 1, and the column observed then that spending a chip before a ball was kicked looked reckless on the Friday and like theft by the Sunday. Two chips gone in two gameweeks and top of the table — it has stopped looking like recklessness and started looking like a plan, though the ammunition is going fast. Jay Curtis took 19 from his own Bench Boost for 111 and sits joint second, which for a man who finished seventeenth last season is quite a statement. Adam Quinney played the same chip for 8 and rather less romance. Elsewhere LUCEROS climbed 11 places from twentieth to ninth without a chip in sight, and Milen Kindekov supplied the week's true differential, Gibbs-White returning 13 for a Pamparam side almost nobody else thought to back.
The Title Race
So the first honest table reads Bluehomie (YouTube) on 191, a lead of 8 and precisely one week of incumbency to defend. Behind, the queue is neither short nor orderly: Rice to meet you and It was actually Eze are level on 183, with Quantum of Tzolis a single point further back on 182 — and Akash Patel has contrived to go from top of the league to fourth without doing very much wrong at all. Four teams inside nine points after two gameweeks is less a title race than a crowded lift. Martin's Team is fifth on 169, 22 adrift, carrying 41 benched points like a rucksack full of bricks. Further down, Son of a Doge — the side that led this league for eighteen weeks last season — has slid from tenth to fourteenth, and a start that was merely unconvincing is beginning to look like a pattern. At the bottom Sporting Club MarRow and Releven Tigers FC are tied on 113, a shared embarrassment rather than a crisis. There are 36 gameweeks left, which is more than enough time for every one of these positions to become a punchline.
The Nerd Corner
The league averaged 98.7 against a global FPL average of 79, a margin of nearly twenty points — and a considerable widening on Gameweek 1, when the same gap was barely five. Before anyone gets comfortable, the template XI scored 109 and only 4 of 23 beat it, against 11 of 23 who cleared it last week. The league therefore comfortably outscored the rest of the world and was comfortably outscored by a squad assembled without a single original thought, which is the sort of paradox that keeps this hobby honest. Defensive contribution remains the quiet arms race, and Milen Kindekov leads it on 8 points, a statistic that wins nothing and explains a great deal. The luck ledger has an early leader in North London Forever, running 22 points above expected points for the season and climbing from fifteenth to sixth on the strength of it — a debt that history tends to call in. Seven chips have now been played in two gameweeks, six of them Bench Boosts, which suggests a league that has collectively decided patience is for other people. Thirty-six weeks remain to find out who was right.
