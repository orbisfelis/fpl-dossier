The Week in Words
GW4 — an editorial nobody asked for
The Disgraces
Consider Darius Goodarzi, who sits second in this league and chose Gameweek 4 to spend his Triple Captain on Palmer, a footballer who responded with 5 points. Tripling five instead of doubling it earned him precisely 5 extra, which is a remarkable return on the most valuable chip in the game, and he compounded it by leaving 22 points on his bench — the week's largest unused pile, from a man who could not afford either mistake. Kris McCarthy took the wooden spoon with 50, fully 26 below a league average of 76.1, and got there by handing the armband to Isak for 4; Sporting Club MarRow has now finished thirteenth, twenty-second, twenty-third and twenty-third, which is less a slump than a settled address. Kelvin Ampene achieved something subtler: Artetecture would have scored 85 by doing absolutely nothing, made transfers, and scored 75 instead, a 10-point fee for the privilege of having an opinion. Claude-Noel Tamakloe went one better and played an entire Wildcard for a net return of -1, which takes a certain commitment. And then there is Jay Curtis, whose It was actually Eze was second after Gameweek 1, second after Gameweek 2 and second again after Gameweek 3 — this column noted the consistency approvingly — and who has now spent a week scoring 64 and arrived in eighth. The most reliable thing in the league has stopped being reliable.
The Shockers
M B scored 114, which is 13 clear of anyone else and the highest single week anybody has managed this season. It is also, in context, faintly absurd: this column spent last week observing that Son of a Doge had gone tenth, sixteenth and seventeenth, that a side which led this league for eighteen weeks last season was hunting for the floor, and that his Wildcard had cost him five points against simply standing still. The Wildcard has now returned 114 — João Pedro captained for 24, Groß for 17, Schade for 15, De Cuyper and Gvardiol for 11 apiece — and carried him 13 places up the table, the sharpest climb of the season by some distance. Evros Stephanou had the better single decision, taking the same João Pedro armband for 24 and pairing it with the transfer of the week, Davis in for O'Nien at a net 14; Calafiori Dreamin' started this season eighteenth and is now fifth. Milen Kindekov posted 101, his best of the campaign, and Pamparam has climbed from twentieth after Gameweek 2 to fourth. Panagiotis Pyrgas got 18 out of a Wildcard and Sunny Sharma 14 from a Free Hit, both perfectly respectable, both entirely drowned out.
The Title Race
Bluehomie (YouTube) remains top on 326 and has now held the summit for three straight weeks, but the lead has been trimmed from 18 to 12, and he managed it with a 78 that barely cleared the league average. Rice to meet you is second on 314 despite the triple-captaincy misadventure, which is either resilience or a warning depending on temperament. Behind them the table has rearranged itself violently: Son of a Doge is third on 308 having been sixteenth a week ago, Pamparam fourth on 304 after climbing ten places, and Quantum of Tzolis and Calafiori Dreamin' are locked together on 301 — the first of those having led this league after the opening weekend and finished first, fourth, fourth and fifth since. Five sides are now within 25 points of the top with 34 gameweeks left, which is close to nothing at all. The bottom is hardening in the other direction, Vigtorious on 216 and Sporting Club MarRow on 201, both of whom have occupied the last two places for three weeks running. The interesting question is no longer whether the leader can be caught but which of the four behind him intends to do it.
The Nerd Corner
The league reasserted itself against the wider world this week: 76.1 against a global average of 69, a 7.1-point win after Gameweek 3's humbling 49.4 against 51. The more uncomfortable number is the template XI, which scored 78 and was beaten by only 8 of 23 managers — down from 12 of 23 a week ago, and, more pointedly, higher than the league's own average. In a week when the obvious picks all delivered, thinking for oneself was a net cost for most of this league. The luck ledger has also changed hands again: Son of a Doge now runs 51 points clear of expected points across the season, comfortably the largest surplus anybody has accumulated, which casts that 114 in a particular light. Two captaincy records remain conspicuous, with North London Forever and Quantum of Tzolis each having produced 2 blank armbands in four attempts. The first genuinely difficult conclusion of the season is that this league's early superiority was built on easy fixtures and generous captaincy, and that the moment both tightened, most of it evaporated.
